# Writing LinkedIn connect notes

LinkedIn caps connection notes at 300 characters. Short, specific and honest works best.

## Template

> Hi [First name], I'm [Your name], a [your role or program] focused on [your field] ([2-4 skills you really have]). [One specific hook about them.] I'd love to connect and learn from [your work / your experience]. Portfolio: https://your-site.example

Only the hook changes per person. A note that could be sent to anyone gets ignored.

## Example (fictional)

> Hi Priya, I'm Sam, a grad student focused on data engineering and analytics (Python, SQL, Spark). Your path from the university's stats program into data engineering at Acme stood out. I'd love to connect and learn from it. Portfolio: https://sam.example

## Hooks by audience

| Audience | Hook |
|---|---|
| Alum or peer | "Your path from [school] into [role] at [company] stood out." |
| Recruiter | "I'd love to follow the work [company]'s data teams do." |
| Hiring manager | "Your work leading [team or area] at [company] stood out." Only if true. |
| Tool-specific contact | "I'm learning [tool] and curious how teams use it in practice." |

## Rules

- Count characters, including the link. Use the full https link.
- Open with a first name and who you are.
- Make the hook specific and true. Do not claim you have followed something you only just read about.
- Ask for one low-pressure thing: to connect and learn. No job, referral or resume ask in the first message.
- Leave out visa status and start dates in the first message.
- Name only skills you actually have.
- Use "Hi Dr. Lastname," for professors and senior leaders. "Dear" is too stiff for LinkedIn.

## After they accept

1. Wait a day or two.
2. Thank them in a short message.
3. Ask one concrete question about their work.
4. Raise openings or referrals only after they have replied.

## If they do not accept

- After 3 to 5 days, send a short email if you have their address.
- After another 3 to 5 days with no reply, mark it as no response and move on.

## Be honest in every message

Do not invent experience, results or interest. If you only learned about something today, say you read about it.
